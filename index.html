The short version

I study how routes, traffic and vehicle technology shape travel time, energy use and emissions, and turn the results into evidence-based recommendations.

I came to transport from electrical and electronic engineering, through an Erasmus Mundus master’s in Sustainable Transport and Electrical Power Systems, and have spent nearly six years in doctoral research at the University of Oviedo. Before that, I taught engineering and led a university department in Bangladesh.

What I bring to a team

  • ModellingUrban and interurban scenarios in PTV Visum, PTV Vissim and Aimsun, checked against published studies and real-driving OBD data.
  • DataRoad geometry, speed limits, signals, elevation and GPS/OBD logs from GIS, OpenStreetMap and routing APIs, collected, cleaned and integrated.
  • DecarbonisationComparisons of conventional, hybrid and electric vehicles on travel time, energy demand and emissions.
  • CommunicationTechnical reports and presentations for multidisciplinary teams; over five years of teaching and three as Head of Department.

The method, as one journeyStage 01 / 05
UrbanMixedInterurban
  1. 1Read
  2. 2Model
  3. 3Test
  4. 4Measure
  5. 5Report
  1. 01

    Read the route

    Every journey starts as data: route geometry and road classification, speed limits, turns, traffic signals, elevation and operating conditions, across urban, mixed and interurban trips.

    • OpenStreetMap
    • Mapbox
    • Geoapify
    • Routing APIs
    • GPS/OBD logs
    • Time series
  2. 02

    Model the traffic

    The cleaned data feeds transport, traffic and vehicle-performance models, built and evaluated in industry simulation software alongside MATLAB and Python.

    • PTV Visum
    • PTV Vissim
    • Aimsun
    • MATLAB
    • Python
  3. 03

    Test the scenarios

    Scenario and sensitivity analyses vary what a driver actually meets on the road, and the assumptions behind the model.

    • Traffic signals
    • Speed profiles
    • Road conditions
    • Driver behaviour
    • Modelling assumptions
  4. 04

    Measure the outcome

    Each run produces comparable indicators for conventional, hybrid and electric vehicles and for different driver profiles, to show what drives performance.

    • Travel time
    • Energy demand
    • Emissions
    • Efficiency
  5. 05

    Check and report

    Results are benchmarked against peer-reviewed studies and established transport and emissions models, with modelled fuel consumption checked against real-driving Torque OBD data, then written up as visualisations, technical reports and evidence-based recommendations.
